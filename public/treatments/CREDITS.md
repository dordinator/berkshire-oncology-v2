## Still to source

No photography is used on the six other treatment pages. This is deliberate
rather than unfinished: the brief asks for equipment photography and real
treatment environments, and explicitly rules out generic technology graphics.
Free-licence libraries have good radiotherapy equipment and almost nothing
usable for drug treatments — a stock photo of an anonymous drip stand or a
gloved hand holding a vial is exactly the generic filler the brief excludes, so
none has been added.

Two candidates were reviewed and rejected:

- **Wikimedia — `Brachytherapy.jpg` (CC BY 4.0).** A live procedure with a
  partially exposed patient and needles in shot. Clinically graphic and not
  appropriate on a page a newly diagnosed patient may read.
- **Wikimedia — `External beam radiotherapy retinoblastoma` (public domain).**
  A 1957 photograph of a named, identifiable child cancer patient. Rejected on
  dignity grounds regardless of licence.

### Shot list, if commissioning or licensing

Ordered by how much each would add. Aspect ratio 3:2, no identifiable patients,
no staged "concerned clinician" stock poses.

1. **Chemotherapy / immunotherapy day unit** — a treatment chair beside a drip
   stand and an infusion pump, room empty, natural light. Answers "what will
   the room be like?", which is the question patients actually ask.
2. **Brachytherapy afterloader** — the equipment alone, no procedure, no
   patient.
3. **Nuclear medicine therapy room** — the room prepared, no patient.
4. **Radiotherapy planning CT / mask fitting** — equipment and immobilisation
   aids only.
5. **Consulting room** — for the hub, if a warmer opening image is wanted.

Suggested paid sources, per the brief: Getty Images, Adobe Stock, Stocksy,
Alamy. Search "radiotherapy department UK", "chemotherapy day unit empty",
"HDR afterloader brachytherapy". Commissioned photography at Spire Dunedin or
GenesisCare would be better than any of them, and would let the captions say
"our" rather than "of the type used".
